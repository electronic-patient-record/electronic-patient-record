# Electronic Patient Record clinical and engineering audit

**25 November 2026 · Recommendation to maintainers and clinical partners — continue with synthetic patients only.** The project can already record, correct, exchange and audit clinically meaningful data. Its most consequential gaps appear where one part of the system hands information to another. A signed result can become critical without reopening review work, a FHIR allergy can mean one thing to a person and another to software, and the chart summary can tell a clinician that stored information does not exist. Fix these before extending the system into prescribing, triage or mobile clinical sign-off.

**Revision and scope.** I audited upstream `main` at `576eca7f`, then compared the relevant paths with the supplied checkout at `1f46b596` (61 commits earlier). The result-routing, observation-range, allergy-code and clock-conversion logic are present in both. The earlier checkout already held results. The problem list, relative history, historical reads and two native mobile scaffolds discussed below are upstream additions. Findings concern synthetic workflows and potential harm. No patient incident, deployment, clinical validation or regulatory approval was established. [Audited revision](https://github.com/electronic-patient-record/electronic-patient-record/tree/576eca7f9a253f1e1b50f90114fb49997334aee4) · [Implementation status](../../../spec/14-implementation-status.md)

## What is worth keeping

- **A correction preserves the original evidence.** A result signature names a version. An amended result can show both the change and the version signed. The service and database also have tests for refusing writes when the audit insert fails. Extend both properties through worklists, exports and recovery. [Result rules](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/electronic-patient-record-domain/src/results.rs) · [Audit failure tests](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/tests/http_audit_refusal.rs)
- **The web UI already has a vocabulary for uncertainty.** The allergy banner distinguishes active allergies, an explicit no-known-allergies statement, an inactive-only history, an unreadable list and an empty record. The summary deliberately retains the newest missing observation with its reason. These are good patterns for problems, medicines and results. [Allergy display](../../../electronic-patient-record-ui-with-svelte/src/lib/allergies.ts) · [Latest reading selection](../../../electronic-patient-record-ui-with-svelte/src/lib/summary.ts)
- **The matcher and service retain evidence for identity decisions.** The matcher explains each field and withholds automatic linkage for a temporary identity. A merge keeps the source rows and records the operator's reason. Tests exercise the permission table against mounted routes. These controls make it possible to investigate mistakes and test a repair. ## Findings to address first

The order reflects potential consequences and the strength of the implementation evidence. It is an engineering prioritisation for clinical review. The project's Clinical Safety Officer (CSO) owns formal hazard judgements.

### 1. Signing an abnormal result can leave a later critical correction without a review task

The patient-level result view correctly marks the corrected version as changed since sign-off. The task engine, however, reduces both abnormal and critical to the same Boolean. Signing can cancel the original task. When the next version changes from abnormal to critical, `raise_for_change` sees `true → true` and emits no filing event. With an approved rule shaped like the supplied example, the old task stays cancelled and the clinician covering the next shift can have nothing in their queue. The supplied rule itself is a draft and creates no task by default. Existing database tests cover cancellation on signing and suppressing duplicate tasks while a task remains open. [Result event logic](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/src/results_store.rs) · [Task facts](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/electronic-patient-record-domain/src/results.rs) · [Draft rule](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/electronic-patient-record-domain/src/task_rules.toml) · [Task store tests](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/tests/tasks_store.rs)

**Recommended change.** Give each review obligation a result version, urgency, accountable recipient and covering recipient. Preserve the reporting service's critical flag. Separate acknowledgement, clinical action and communication to the patient. The laboratory and receiving team should agree how a critical result reaches a person outside working hours, including after discharge. The SAFER guide specifically addresses acknowledgement and escalation when the intended recipient is unavailable. [ASTP SAFER Guide, Test Results Reporting and Follow-Up, practice 2.5](https://healthit.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/SAFER-Guide-8.-Test-Results-Reporting-Final.pdf)

**Acceptance.** File an abnormal result, sign version 1, hand over, then correct it to critical. The covering clinician receives exactly one open obligation for version 2. Replay creates no duplicate. The earlier signature remains visible and cannot satisfy the new obligation. Let the locally agreed acknowledgement time expire and verify delivery to the backup. Carry this case into OR-4, IW-5, H-050 and H-059.

### 2. An empty reference range can turn into a normal observation

I passed the same synthetic heart rate, 130/min, through the checked-out Rust domain functions. These values test software boundaries and have no intended clinical interpretation. All three inputs passed validation. [Observation validation, flags and task facts](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/electronic-patient-record-domain/src/observations.rs)

| Reference supplied | Critical limit supplied | Returned flag | Out-of-range event predicate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Empty object | Absent | Normal | False |
| Absent | Upper 120 | No flag | False |
| Upper 100 | Upper 120 | Critical high | True |

The web form omits a range when the user leaves both boxes blank. An API client can supply the empty object. The FHIR mapper can then export interpretation `N` while omitting the empty reference range. In the second case a real limit supplied by the sender is crossed, yet the task predicate is false. The existing unit test explicitly specifies that a critical range alone flags nothing. [Web form conversion](../../../electronic-patient-record-ui-with-svelte/src/lib/vitals.ts) · [Observation store](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/src/observations_store.rs) · [FHIR mapping](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/src/interop/fhir_observation.rs)

**Acceptance.** Treat a boundless reference as absent on reads of stored data and reject or normalise it on new writes. Agree with the clinical owner how to handle a critical-only limit. Prefer evaluating an exceeded, valid critical bound independently while leaving the ordinary reference assessment unknown. Exercise omitted, empty, one-sided and crossed bounds through API, chart, task and FHIR. No case lacking a usable reference may produce an ordinary normal interpretation. Review the service's hard plausibility limits with the intended care setting as a separate H-034 decision.

### 3. A passing FHIR allergy fixture pairs penicillin with the code for myocardial infarction

The R4 mapping test asserts both “Penicillin” and SNOMED CT `22298006` on the same allergy. The repository's own terminology fixture identifies that concept as myocardial infarction. The external-validator fixture repeats the pair. The input validator checks identifier shape and component type. A receiving system that indexes the coding and a pharmacist reading the text could therefore act on different meanings. This is observable in the fixtures and supported by the write and export code. No live transmission was examined. [Mapping and test](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/src/interop/fhir_allergy.rs) · [Terminology fixture](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/src/terminology.rs) · [Input check](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/src/interop/terminology.rs) · [Validator fixture](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/tests/fhir_validator.rs) · [HL7 R4 CodeableConcept](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/datatypes.html#CodeableConcept)

**Acceptance.** Retain this mismatch as a negative case. Build a positive case with a pharmacist-reviewed substance, a pinned terminology edition and an appropriate allergen value set. Accept recognised synonyms and preserve the reporter's words. Keep an unverified report visible to people with its provenance and uncertainty, and withhold an unverified coding from automated medication checks. Demonstrate with a receiving system that it resolves the intended substance and learns of a later correction or withdrawal. A previously exported allergy marked entered in error is omitted by the current mapper. A consumer that keeps prior records needs an agreed reconciliation mechanism.

**Clinical improvement.** Record who supplied the allergy history, when it was checked, whether staff could ascertain it, and the manifestations and severity of the actual reaction. Preserve partial dates and distinguish that past reaction's severity from the estimated danger of a future exposure. Make the confirmed history available during medicines reconciliation and at discharge. Link RC-5, RC-7 and RC-10 to H-018 and H-022. [Allergy record fields](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/src/allergies.rs) · [NICE CG183, recommendations 1.2.1–1.2.8](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg183/chapter/Recommendations) · [HL7 R4 AllergyIntolerance](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/allergyintolerance.html)

### 4. Correcting a vital sign can silently move the measurement time

The correction form converts the original instant to a minute-resolution wall time, then converts it back on every save. Running the actual helpers with Node in the `Europe/London` zone turned `2026-10-25T00:30:45Z` into wall time `01:30` and back into `2026-10-25T01:30:00Z`. A value-only edit can therefore move the measurement almost an hour later in a deterioration timeline. On an ordinary day the round trip also drops seconds. Entering the nonexistent London time `2026-03-29 01:30` produced an instant displayed locally as `02:30`. [Correction form](../../../electronic-patient-record-ui-with-svelte/src/lib/VitalsSection.svelte) · [Time conversion](../../../electronic-patient-record-ui-with-svelte/src/lib/vitals.ts)

**Acceptance.** Preserve the original instant, including its precision, whenever the user edits only the value. On an explicit time edit, reject a skipped wall time and offer both offsets for a repeated one. Test both autumn instants, the spring gap and an ordinary timestamp with fractional seconds through save, reread, trend and FHIR export. Keep measured and recorded times independently visible.

### 5. The patient summary says stored clinical information does not exist

The summary puts “Recent results” and “Problems” beneath “Not built in this system yet” and says “Nothing is held for these.” A clinician can already file a result. The audited upstream service can also store a confirmed problem. A receiving team could reasonably stop looking at the summary during handover. The older checkout already has this discrepancy for results. The low-cost immediate fix is accurate copy even before another clinical section is built. [Summary labels](../../../electronic-patient-record-ui-with-svelte/src/lib/strings.ts) · [Summary screen](../../../electronic-patient-record-ui-with-svelte/src/routes/patients/%5Bid%5D/summary/+page.svelte) · [Results controller](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/src/controllers/results.rs) · [Upstream problem-list contract](https://github.com/electronic-patient-record/electronic-patient-record/blob/576eca7f9a253f1e1b50f90114fb49997334aee4/spec/problem-list/index.md)

**Acceptance.** State that the summary does not display those sections. Give authorised staff a route to the source when one exists. For integrated sections, distinguish loading, unavailable, access restricted, empty after a successful read, and confirmed absence. Preserve diagnostic certainty and current status. File a synthetic result and provisional problem, open the summary as an entitled clinician and a restricted reader, then repeat during a service failure. Check the wording with users, including Welsh speakers, and avoid revealing the existence of protected entries.

### 6. A merged chart's access-log entry can depend on which observation sorts first

The observation list authorises a read of the requested patient and returns rows from linked identities, ordered by measurement time. It takes the patient reference for the audit event from the first returned row. The patient-facing log filters by one exact patient reference. If the newest observation belongs to a merged-in identity, the survivor's log query misses that list-read event. The allergy list also selects the audit patient from the first row. A valid chain digest can thus protect an event whose attribution gives an incomplete account of who opened the chart. This is a source trace, without a database replay in this review. [Observation list controller](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/src/controllers/observations.rs) · [Observation ordering](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/src/observations_store.rs) · [Allergy list controller](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/src/controllers/allergies.rs) · [Patient log query](../../../electronic-patient-record-service-with-rust/src/audit.rs)

**Acceptance.** Record the requested chart as the access context and retain the contributing records' provenance for authorised investigation. Have the privacy lead define patient and proxy visibility after reversal of a mistaken merge. Test a mixed list in both sort orders, an empty list, a subsequent unmerge and both patient-facing and auditor views. The event for opening the survivor's chart should remain discoverable without disclosing another person's subsequent activity.

## Delivery sequence and evidence boundary

1. **First engineering increment.** Fix the incorrect summary text and allergy fixture, then add failing regression cases for findings 1–6. Give each change its own spec, implementation, test and applicable hazard update. Keep the mismatch fixture as a test that should fail clinical validation.
2. **First clinical simulation.** Run one result from filing to correction, shift handover, escalation and patient communication with synthetic patients. Include a patient identity ambiguity and a system interruption. Ask the receiving clinician to show what remains their responsibility. Record missed or duplicate obligations, time to acknowledgement and who acted. Investigate every missed critical correction before expanding scope. [Existing clinical safety measures](../../../spec/clinical-safety/measurement.md)
3. **Before any real-patient use.** Have the responsible site review a witnessed restore, partner reconciliation, identity evaluation, access decisions, accessibility findings and the clinical safety case for the specific function. A future clinical decision-support feature such as triage or dosage advice needs its own intended-use and risk assessment. Keep the present synthetic-use restriction in force. [Production evidence checklist](../../../governance/production-readiness.md)

**Checks performed.** On the audited revision, the locked offline matcher suite passed 133 tests (7 library and 126 integration). The locked domain library suite passed 237. I ran the hazard-log checker with `--report`, exercised the observation cases above against the actual domain crate, and ran the unmodified time-conversion functions from `vitals.ts` under Node 26.13.1. I traced the other findings through source, tests and specifications. External guidance informed the recommendations. The US SAFER guides are used here as workflow design references.

**Limits.** This was a targeted repository audit. I did not run the database-backed suite, browser or device journeys, the external FHIR validator, load tests, a restore, or an interface partner trial. There were no clinical user interviews, records of patient outcomes, or site-specific configurations to assess. The code establishes what the reviewed paths can do. The proposed acceptance tests and clinical exercises identify the evidence still needed.
